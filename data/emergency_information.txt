WHAT CONSTITUTES A MEDICAL EMERGENCY?
=========================================================
A medical emergency is any situation where a person's life, health,
or bodily function is at immediate risk and requires urgent medical
attention.

CALL 112 OR +234 800 MEDCARE IMMEDIATELY IF YOU OR SOMEONE ELSE
IS EXPERIENCING ANY OF THE FOLLOWING:

CARDIAC EMERGENCIES:
  - Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes
  - Pain radiating to the arm, neck, jaw, or back
  - Shortness of breath with chest discomfort
  - Sudden sweating, nausea, or lightheadedness with chest pain
  - Palpitations with dizziness or fainting

STROKE EMERGENCIES (Remember "FAST"):
  - F (Face): Sudden drooping or numbness on one side of the face
  - A (Arms): Sudden weakness or numbness in one arm
  - S (Speech): Sudden slurred speech or difficulty speaking
  - T (Time): Time to call emergency services immediately
  - Also: Sudden confusion, vision problems, severe headache,
    loss of balance or coordination

TRAUMA AND ACCIDENTS:
  - Major vehicle accidents
  - Falls from height
  - Severe cuts or lacerations with heavy bleeding
  - Broken bones with visible deformity
  - Head injuries with loss of consciousness
  - Spinal injuries or neck pain after trauma

RESPIRATORY EMERGENCIES:
  - Severe difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  - Choking or inability to breathe
  - Severe asthma attack not responding to inhaler
  - Bluish lips or face
  - Stridor (high-pitched breathing sound)

OBSTETRIC EMERGENCIES:
  - Heavy vaginal bleeding during pregnancy
  - Severe abdominal pain during pregnancy
  - Premature labor (before 37 weeks)
  - Water breaking before term
  - No fetal movement for an extended period
  - Severe headache, vision changes, or swelling in late pregnancy
    (possible pre-eclampsia)

PEDIATRIC EMERGENCIES:
  - High fever with stiff neck or rash
  - Seizures or convulsions
  - Difficulty breathing or blue lips
  - Severe dehydration (no tears, dry mouth, no urination)
  - Ingestion of poison or foreign objects
  - Unresponsiveness or extreme lethargy

OTHER MEDICAL EMERGENCIES:
  - Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) with swelling,
    difficulty breathing, or hives
  - Poisoning or drug overdose
  - Severe burns
  - Electric shock
  - Drowning or near-drowning
  - Suicidal thoughts or self-harm
  - Sudden severe headache ("worst headache of my life")
  - Sudden vision loss
  - Uncontrolled bleeding
  - Diabetic emergency (very high or very low blood sugar
    with confusion or unconsciousness)

WHAT IS NOT A MEDICAL EMERGENCY?
=========================================================
The following conditions generally do NOT require emergency services
and should be addressed through regular outpatient appointments:

  - Minor cuts or scrapes that can be cleaned at home
  - Mild fever without other serious symptoms
  - Common cold or mild flu symptoms
  - Minor sprains or strains
  - Routine medication refills
  - Scheduled follow-up appointments
  - Minor rashes without breathing difficulties
  - Earaches or sore throats without severe symptoms
  - Routine health screenings
  - Non-urgent dental problems

For these conditions, please:
  - Book an appointment through our website or by calling
    +234 800 MEDCARE during reception hours
  - Visit the General Medicine department as a walk-in patient
  - Contact your regular physician

HOW TO REQUEST AN AMBULANCE
=========================================================
MedCare Specialist Hospital provides ambulance services for
emergency patient transport.

TO REQUEST AN AMBULANCE:

Step 1: Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE immediately

Step 2: Provide the following information clearly:
  - Your full name
  - Your exact location (address, landmarks, floor, room number)
  - Nature of the emergency (what happened, symptoms)
  - Number of people requiring assistance
  - Age and gender of the patient(s)
  - Any known medical conditions or allergies
  - Whether the patient is conscious and breathing

Step 3: Follow any instructions given by the emergency operator

Step 4: Stay on the line until told to hang up

Step 5: If possible, have someone wait outside to guide
        the ambulance to the patient

AMBULANCE SERVICE HOURS:
  Available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week

AMBULANCE FEES:
  Ambulance services may incur a fee depending on the distance
  and type of service required. The emergency operator will
  inform you of any applicable charges. In life-threatening
  emergencies, transport will not be delayed due to payment.

WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING FOR AN AMBULANCE
=========================================================

GENERAL GUIDELINES:
  - Stay calm and keep the patient calm
  - Do not hang up until the operator tells you to
  - Follow all instructions given by the emergency operator
  - Gather the patient's medications, medical records, and ID
  - Unlock doors and clear a path for emergency responders
  - Have someone wait outside to guide the ambulance

FOR UNCONSCIOUS PATIENTS:
  - Check if the patient is breathing
  - If not breathing, begin CPR if you are trained
  - If breathing, place the patient in the recovery position
    (on their side with head tilted back)
  - Do not give food, drink, or medication
  - Monitor breathing until help arrives

FOR SEVERE BLEEDING:
  - Apply direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth
    or bandage
  - Elevate the injured area above heart level if possible
  - Do not remove embedded objects
  - Continue pressure until help arrives

FOR SUSPECTED HEART ATTACK:
  - Have the patient sit or lie down in a comfortable position
  - Loosen tight clothing
  - If the patient has prescribed nitroglycerin, help them
    take it as directed
  - If the patient is not allergic and has no contraindications,
    they may chew one adult aspirin (325mg) while waiting
  - Be prepared to perform CPR if the patient becomes
    unresponsive

FOR SUSPECTED STROKE:
  - Note the time when symptoms first appeared (critical for
    treatment decisions)
  - Do not give the patient anything to eat or drink
  - Do not give aspirin or other medications
  - Keep the patient comfortable and monitor breathing
  - If vomiting occurs, turn the patient on their side

FOR CHOKING:
  - If the person can cough or speak, encourage them to
    continue coughing
  - If they cannot breathe, speak, or cough, perform the
    Heimlich maneuver (abdominal thrusts) if trained
  - For infants, use back slaps and chest thrusts
  - Call for emergency help immediately

FOR SEIZURES:
  - Clear the area of hard or sharp objects
  - Place something soft under the patient's head
  - Turn the patient on their side after the seizure ends
  - Do not restrain the patient or put anything in their mouth
  - Time the seizure duration
  - Call emergency services if the seizure lasts more than
    5 minutes or if the patient has repeated seizures

FOR BURNS:
  - Cool the burn with cool (not ice-cold) running water
    for at least 10-20 minutes
  - Remove jewelry or tight clothing near the burn before
    swelling occurs
  - Cover the burn with a clean, non-stick dressing
  - Do not apply ice, butter, oil, or ointments
  - Do not break blisters

FOR POISONING:
  - Call emergency services immediately
  - Do not induce vomiting unless instructed by a professional
  - If the poison is on the skin, rinse with water for
    at least 15 minutes
  - If inhaled, move the person to fresh air
  - Have the poison container available to show medical staff

=========================================================
TRIAGE PROCESS
=========================================================
Upon arrival at the Emergency Department, patients are assessed
through a triage system to prioritize care based on the severity
of their condition.

TRIAGE CATEGORIES:

1. RED (IMMEDIATE / LIFE-THREATENING)
   - Patients requiring immediate life-saving intervention
   - Examples: Cardiac arrest, severe trauma, stroke,
     severe respiratory distress, major bleeding
   - Seen immediately upon arrival

2. ORANGE (VERY URGENT)
   - Patients with potentially life-threatening conditions
     requiring rapid assessment
   - Examples: Chest pain, moderate trauma, severe abdominal
     pain, high fever with altered consciousness
   - Seen within 10-15 minutes

3. YELLOW (URGENT)
   - Patients with conditions that could worsen without
     timely treatment
   - Examples: Moderate asthma, fractures without severe
     bleeding, moderate dehydration, minor head injury
   - Seen within 30-60 minutes

4. GREEN (LESS URGENT)
   - Patients with non-life-threatening conditions
   - Examples: Minor cuts, mild sprains, low-grade fever,
     minor rashes
   - Seen within 1-2 hours

5. BLUE (NON-URGENT)
   - Patients who may be better served by outpatient care
   - Examples: Medication refills, routine follow-ups,
     minor ailments
   - May be referred to outpatient services

IMPORTANT:
  - Triage is based on medical need, not order of arrival
  - A patient who arrives later but has a more severe
    condition will be seen first
  - Please be patient and trust the triage process
  - If your condition worsens while waiting, inform the
    nursing staff immediately

EMERGENCY PROCEDURES FOR SPECIFIC SITUATIONS
=========================================================

CARDIAC ARREST:
  1. Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE immediately
  2. Check for responsiveness and breathing
  3. If not breathing or only gasping, begin CPR:
     - Place hands on center of chest
     - Push hard and fast (100-120 compressions per minute)
     - Allow chest to recoil between compressions
  4. Use an AED if available
  5. Continue until emergency help arrives

SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION (ANAPHYLAXIS):
  1. Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE immediately
  2. If the patient has an epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen),
     help them use it immediately
  3. Lay the person flat and elevate their legs
  4. Do not give anything by mouth
  5. If breathing stops, begin CPR
  6. Stay with the person until help arrives

HEAT STROKE:
  1. Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE
  2. Move the person to a cool, shaded area
  3. Remove excess clothing
  4. Cool the person with whatever means available:
     - Cool water immersion
     - Wet sheets and fanning
     - Ice packs on neck, armpits, and groin
  5. Do not give fluids if the person is unconscious
  6. Monitor breathing and be ready to perform CPR

HYPOTHERMIA:
  1. Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE
  2. Move the person to a warm, dry location
  3. Remove wet clothing and replace with dry, warm layers
  4. Warm the person gradually:
     - Use blankets
     - Apply warm (not hot) compresses to chest, neck, groin
     - Do NOT use direct heat (hot water, heating pads)
  5. If conscious, give warm (not hot) non-alcoholic drinks
  6. Monitor breathing and be ready to perform CPR

  - Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments

PREPARING FOR EMERGENCIES
=========================================================
Being prepared can save lives. We recommend the following
emergency preparedness measures:

AT HOME:
  - Keep a well-stocked first aid kit
  - Post emergency numbers near all phones
  - Teach family members basic first aid and CPR
  - Know the location of the nearest hospital
  - Keep a list of medications and allergies accessible
  - Have a plan for emergencies (who to call, how to
    get to the hospital)

IN YOUR VEHICLE:
  - Keep a basic first aid kit in your car
  - Have your phone charged and accessible
  - Know your location when calling for help
  - Keep emergency contact information in your vehicle

FOR CHRONIC CONDITIONS:
  - Always carry a list of your medications and dosages
  - Wear a medical alert bracelet if you have serious
    allergies or conditions
  - Keep emergency medications (e.g., inhaler, nitroglycerin,
    epinephrine) with you at all times
  - Inform family members about your condition and
    emergency plan

FOR ELDERLY FAMILY MEMBERS:
  - Ensure they have a way to call for help (phone,
    medical alert device)
  - Keep a list of their medications and conditions
    easily accessible
  - Inform neighbors or nearby family about their
    health status
  - Consider regular check-in calls or visits

FOR CHILDREN:
  - Teach children how to call 112 in an emergency
  - Make sure they know their full name and address
  - Keep poison control information accessible
  - Childproof your home to prevent accidents
  - Supervise children around water, roads, and hazards

EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT VISITOR POLICY
=========================================================
To ensure the safety and privacy of emergency patients,
the following visitor policies apply to the Emergency
Department:

  - Only ONE (1) companion is allowed with each patient
    at any given time
  - Additional family members must wait in the designated
    waiting area
  - Visitors may be asked to leave during certain procedures
    or if the patient's condition requires it
  - Children under 12 are generally not permitted in the
    emergency treatment areas
  - All visitors must follow instructions from emergency
    staff at all times
  - Visitors showing signs of illness may be asked to
    leave to protect vulnerable patients
  - Photography and recording are strictly prohibited
    in the Emergency Department

FREQUENTLY ASKED EMERGENCY QUESTIONS
=========================================================

Q: Will I be treated if I cannot pay for emergency care?
A: Yes. MedCare Specialist Hospital will NEVER deny
   emergency treatment due to inability to pay. Life-saving
   care will be provided immediately. Billing and payment
   arrangements will be addressed after you are stabilized.

Q: How long will I have to wait in the Emergency Department?
A: Wait times vary depending on the severity of your
   condition and the number of patients. Patients with
   life-threatening conditions are seen immediately.
   Less urgent cases may experience longer wait times.
   Please be patient and trust the triage process.

Q: Can I leave the Emergency Department before being seen?
A: You have the right to leave, but we strongly advise
   against it if you are experiencing serious symptoms.
   Leaving against medical advice can be dangerous.
   If you must leave, please inform the nursing staff
   and sign a discharge against medical advice form.

Q: Can I drive myself to the hospital in an emergency?
A: For serious emergencies (chest pain, stroke symptoms,
   severe trauma, difficulty breathing), you should NOT
   drive yourself. Call 112 or +234 800 MEDCARE for an
   ambulance. Emergency medical personnel can begin
   treatment en route and ensure your safety during
   transport.

Q: What if I need emergency care at night or on a
   public holiday?
A: The Emergency Department is open 24 hours a day,
   7 days a week, including all nights, weekends, and
   public holidays. Emergency care is always available.

Q: Can I bring my own medications to the Emergency
   Department?
A: Yes. Please bring all medications you are currently
   taking, including over-the-counter drugs and supplements.
   This helps our medical team avoid drug interactions
   and make informed treatment decisions.

Q: Will the Emergency Department notify my family?
A: With your permission, we will notify your emergency
   contact. If you are unable to communicate, we will
   make reasonable efforts to contact your next of kin.
   Please ensure your emergency contact information is
   up to date.

Q: Can I request a specific doctor in the Emergency
   Department?
A: Emergency care is provided by the on-duty emergency
   medical team. While we cannot guarantee a specific
   doctor, all our emergency physicians are qualified
   and experienced in handling medical emergencies.
   Specialist consultation will be arranged as needed.

Q: What happens after I am stabilized in the Emergency
   Department?
A: Depending on your condition, you may be:
   - Discharged home with follow-up instructions
   - Admitted to a hospital ward for further care
   - Transferred to a specialized facility if needed
   - Referred to an outpatient specialist for follow-up

Q: How do I file a complaint about Emergency Department
   services?
A: We take all feedback seriously. To file a complaint:
   - Speak with the patient support officer
   - Call +234 800 MEDCARE during reception hours
   - Email support@medcarespecialist.com with
     "Emergency Department Complaint" in the subject line
   All complaints are reviewed and responded to within
   five (5) working days.
